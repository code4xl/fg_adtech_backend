Night Markets Return as Old Shipping Warehouses Find New Life

For decades, the eastern pier was a silent row of corrugated doors and rusted cranes. This month, it reopened as a lantern-lit promenade where families wander between food stalls, live jazz, and pop-up bookstores that spill their titles out onto the dock.

City planners say the project is part of a broader experiment in “soft redevelopment” — changing how a neighborhood feels before changing what it costs. Vendors sign short seasonal leases, and residents were invited to co-design the layout of the market.

On opening night, elders who once worked the loading lines posed for photos under the same warehouse numbers where they had once punched the clock.

Harbor by the numbers

• 14 warehouses converted for public space this year
• 2.3 km of continuous waterfront now open to pedestrians
• 4,800 jobs estimated across food, arts, and logistics support

“We wanted the docks to feel like a living room for the whole city,” said the project architect. “The sea is our oldest neighbor — we’re just inviting it back in.”
